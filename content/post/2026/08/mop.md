oh man, accounting style software is a pain in the ass.

So, I've got this app right?  R3r4um told you about this in his roadmap post in May.  I'll assume you're familiar with crafting things in games and EVE is the mother of all crafting solutions, lol.  There are 3 'tiers' of production in EVE Online - Tech 1 (T1), Tech 2 (T2) and Tech 3 (T3).  But T3 ships are basically a tier of their own and are completely disconnected from T2 and T1.  T1 is obviously the simplest of the builds, all T1 things require minimal materials and they're generally raw materials.  So you mine some stuff, refine that stuff and build with that stuff.  Simple right?  Ok, so T2 (except ammo) builds on T1.  So a T2 ship will basically add stuff to a T1 ship so you need the T1 ship as part of the build.  BUT, you need all of these additions as well which are composed of what we'll simply call 'components'.  To build these components you need the output of a reaction formula performed in a station in not very safe space right?  There are 2 layers of reactions and the first layer consumes raw moon materials and ice products via fuel cells which are manufactured.  Unlike T1 materials which you can find in any solar system, moon materials only come from systems that have a .5 or lower security status and .5 specifically gives you only the most common of moon materials.  You have to go into .4 and below space to get the good stuff and the cops don't go there... so it's generally a blood bath right?  So... we live in a realm where we buy raw moon mats off the market, we're completely disconnected from the fights that spawn on these moons and merely see price fluctuations based on availability and cartels that withhold their full stockpile....  that's a lot of words so far... but that's the very basic of what we're working with.

So, this application is all about finding production opportunities and paying people involves using a profit sharing model.  So every 5 hours this thing wakes up and performs thousands of calculations and build tree compilations to find items that can be manufactured and sold for a profit.  This thing calculates the entire build chain, from T2 ship all the way down to how to build the fuel that is consumed in the reactions and then puts market prices behind it all and displays all this data in a searchable screen so you can start to get these assigned to people...

It's next function is to get people involved in building these things so it has an assignment function.  That then translates into having ways to show these people what they're assigned and also give them progress of their ingame jobs and how each of the jobs relates to an assignment.

Next up goes into the accounting functions... so a completed build translates into what I'm calling a 'sell obligation' and these capture information like the sell price of the item at the time of completion, the estimated build cost of that item, how many of that item were actually done and who built it.  Every sale that comes into the system that has an active sell obligation creates a sales receipt which captures how much it was sold for, the tax spent, the brokers fee spent and who and where it was sold (along with who bought it but we're not using that information yet).  Tax and brokers fee's are currently estimations based on max skills but will soon exactly match reality.  Soon as I sit down and break down how to find the right transactions... I'm looking at you brokers fee...

So that catches you up to where I currently am with the people that are generating data for me.  So, now I'm working out how to get these people paid and still make money for the corporation to buy more blueprints and fund things for the corp etc.

So, this new function will create liabilities for each receipt.  These liabilities obviously represent money that will soon leave our sales coffers.  You put together a policy which defines how much of the profit goes where.  Either to the builder, the seller, someone specific, or a budget category with a forced 'remainder' category which I should rename to 'buffer' and use that to eat up any inaccuracies in the estimations that happened upstream which don't match reality.  This is where I currently am on my own dev instance of the app which syncs up with the live instance from time to time so I have data to play with that no one see's so I don't disrupt people at all with deploy after deploy after deploy, sometimes in rapid succession...

My next task is to produce a UI that will allow a 'budget coordinator' to build up payment batches.  So, these batches will hoover up all the liabilities and aggregate them into just a few specific buckets... the budget categories I just talked about.  So instead of thousands of little liabilities (477 sales events translates to about 5500 liabilities with my current setup) we end up with like 8 line items and 1 line item per person receiving money.  This then tells someone how to manually transfer corporation cash to the bucket or the person in question.

After that I'll provide a reconciliation feature that will double check this manual step and provide 'proofs' of money paid by inspecting the wallet journal we hoover up from the game every hour.  Phew, breath and relax :D.
