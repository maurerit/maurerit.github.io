I just came home with a new MacBook Neo yesterday.  It was a little touch and go getting one, we went to 3 places only to find they were out of stock but then we checked the Target near our home and they were in stock with at least a Silver.  While I would have preferred the citrus or space grey, I really just wanted a couch/travel computer.  I'd been looking forward to computing on the couch since we ordered this thing and then the laptop goes kaput after just a week or so.  Just as I was getting settled in :(.

So I've had to make some adjustments but everything is falling into place.  The display at first was configured with 'default spacing' and WTF Apple?  I couldn't even see the entire Amazon.com menu things were that big.  I was really doubting the specs of this screen.  I dealt with it for a few hours as I was settling in until I ran across the setting for 'space' in Displays.  Looking at these options I have an effective resolution of 1637x1024?  So weird.  Eh, it looks sexy :D.

Some of the adjustments I've had to make, and silly as it sounds, is not having wobbly windows.  Yes, something as simple as that just makes my day.  I don't mess around with it often but whenever I do move a window... I give it a little jiggle and laugh that someone actually implemented that.  I've loved that for years and years.  Such hilarious waste of time but that's what makes open source beautiful.

The next was how my multiple workspace work.  On linux I've told you all that I have 9 workspaces.  Many of which are employed when I'm really getting busy.  They're layed out in a square, 3x3 and each space has it's purpose.  It's perfect for constrained space computing when you're always on the keyboard/touchpad.  Simple 3 finger gestures and you're on the app you want and the muscle memory comes quick.  Apple's virtual desktops... they transition smoothly... that's the only positive I have to say about that :D.  I'll manage though, I rather like the dock on macOS... so I'll move my workflow to use that a bit more since ALL apps display down there regardless of being visible in the current desktop.

That's the main adjustments I've had to make so I think that's pretty minimal.  I can tolerate that just fine.  What I do want to know though is if this can open me up to some light iOS development or if that'll be a bit too heavy for xcode... maybe develop in VS Code and use cli tools?  I'll probably look into that because I wouldn't mind doing some light iOS apps... but then I'd have to debate on if it's work 100 bucks a year to publish to the store and deal with that hassle... but I digress, I'll complain about the walled garden later ;).

You do have to know what you're getting into when you get into a Neo.  It's a rather constrained computing environment for modern day use.  It will serve the purpose of maybe 90% of people in the world but the other 10%... the super heavy computer users will not be happy with it unless they expectation set accordingly.  I'm pushing this thing to it's max where I have ~2-3g of compressed memory and ~6g of usage otherwise reported.  I haven't dug into how Activity Monitor reports but what I'm thinking is that I have 3g of compressed memory, 3g of regular memory and 3g of swap.  Not too terrible.  That's about my lightweight computing footprint on linux (usually more like 12g) so I'm happy so far.

The size is perfect, full size 60% keyboard, fits on the lap, is super light and thin and the keys are like what they used to be before the butterfly switch.  What a fiasco that was.  Such a terrible typing experience too.  At least these chiclets have a special feel to them.  They feel better than most windows laptops I've used in the past and currently.  Maybe I'm a fanboy in denial... well... no screw that... I complain about them too much... BUT I LOVE THEIR HARDWARE... but I'd love it more if I could Artix on it :P.  But alas, I don't think we've cracked the A18 chip yet, maybe it'll be easier than the M4/5?  I don't know, I'll have to watch the Asahi space to see if they try to get themselves on Neo's.

The battery life, I still need to 'break the battery in' but the first day after the first charge I managed to get 7 hours and still had 33% battery left.  I'll be keeping an eye on that over the next few days.  My ideal situation is I have my work computer with me (which is a nice lightweight Dell Precision) which needs a new battery but I have a charger out here that's capable of at least giving it some power.  While it's not the fondest of that 100w source it deals with it and chugs right along.  I want me Neo with me as well for short breaks where I can maybe do something with my Agents to move home tasks forward while I work.  So the Neo will be powered on all day and then all night while I browse and read my news.  It'll be a good setup I feel.

For now, I'm highly satisfied with my decision.  We'll see how repairable these ACTUALLY are once Apple does something to get their parts listed.
